## Physics

**Every figure in this file that the simulation produced is a record, not a reference.** Test
against real-world physics only: conservation, analytic solutions, published data. A reading
quoted here cannot pass or fail anything, because the code that printed it has had bugs.

**The target is BEHAVIOUR, not a decimal.** This will never simulate a plant one-to-one. A job that
ends by chasing the last few per cent of a published figure has failed, not succeeded. Where a check
compares a decimal, ask first whether the published source states a behaviour instead, and compare
that.

### The engine as it stands (18/09/26)

The tick was rewritten into `src/eng/` (flat state, integer tables; `docs/architecture.md`). Every
function name in the sections below that is not found in `src/` belongs to the deleted engine: the
physics it named lives in `src/eng/` under the `e`-prefixed name (`advectStep()` → `eAdvectStep()`,
`roomGasStep()` → `eGasStep()`, `liqStep()` → `eLiqStep()`, `coreStep()` → `eCoreStep()`,
`tubeStep()` → `eTubeStep()`, `springStep()` → `eSpringStep()`, `potStep()` → the per-machine pots in
`machines.js`), its constants as `E_*`, and `s.<field>` is `ST.<field>` / `ST.sc[SC_<NAME>]`. Where the
model itself changed with the rewrite, this section is the statement and the older text is history:

- **Water and steam are IAPWS-IF97, at their own pressure.** The saturation line is region 4; h_f,
  h_g, rho_f and rho_g along it are regions 1 to 3 at psat (`WL_*`). Single-phase water is one table
  built at load off regions 1, 2 and 3 and the B23 line (`WT_*`, `pipenet.js`): pressure rows from
  611.657 Pa to 100 MPa with one exactly at 22.064 MPa, every row sampled at the same absolute enthalpy
  nodes (T and rho, read by `mixA()`, `tOfHA()`, `tLiqA()`) and the same temperature nodes (h, read by
  `hOfTP()`; c_p is its secant, `cpOfTP()`). Below pc a row keeps a liquid and a vapour side, each carried
  into metastable water far enough to cover the saturation line up to its neighbouring rows, so a read
  between two rows at one h is the secant of the true isenthalp and the compressibility (`kapA()`) is the
  one the density implies. Above pc the branch label follows the pseudo-critical line (`wHpc()`, the
  table's c_p peak): liquid below it, steam above, rho and T continuous. `hOfT()` stays for a temperature
  that is a saturation temperature. No water `COOLANT` row states rho, Ts, h_fg or c_p: `coolFig()`
  derives them from IF97 at the row's own P0 and Tref, c_p as the secant over its rise (capped at h_f).
  A boiling row states its core exit quality `xOut` instead of a rise (`coolBoils()`): the feed at
  the plant's feed temperature (`feedTOf()`) mixes into the separated water, so h_in = h_f − x(h_f − h_feed), dT0 is derived, and the
  rated flow is Q/(x(h_g − h_feed)) (`coreRatedKgs()`, the one door for every rated flow).
  Past 1073.15 K (region 5) and outside the rows' pressures it is a stated gap (`docs/fidelity.md`).
- **Decay heat is ANSI/ANS-5.1-1979's own 23 groups** for thermal fission of U-235 after infinite
  irradiation, each group updated by an exact exponential over the tick (`E_DEC_ANS`, `E_DEC_A`,
  `E_DEC_L`). Their sum is scaled to `1 − PROMPT_F`, 1.4 % under the standard's own total, and that is
  the whole error from 1 s to 3.5 days. The 11-group Way-Wigner fit it replaced read 21 % low at 10 s
  and 30 % high at 1e5 s.
- **Not all the fission heat goes through the pin, and the gammas are transported (22/09/26).** Neutron
  slowing-down energy lands where the moderation is; fragments and delayed betas stay in the fuel; the
  fission gammas are born where fission is and the capture gammas where the captures are (the thermal
  absorption book, `latBook()`), in five photon groups on NIST's per-group μ/ρ and μ_en/ρ, with Compton
  down-scatter off Klein–Nishina. The partition is ENDF/B-VIII.0's evaluated fission energy release
  (MF=1 MT=458) plus the drawing's own capture energy (`heatCellOf()`, `gamDepose()`, `heatPointA()`,
  `heatSplitA()`). The regions are fuel, can, coolant, blocks, pressure tube, drawn absorber, control
  channel water and control channel tube. The drawn fuel slot is a 2D cell (`gamCellOf()`: a pin cell for
  a bare slot, the whole slot with its rods laid in rings for a bored one, salt alone for a dissolved
  fuel), its first-flight collision probabilities tracked exactly with Bickley's Ki3 and a white edge
  (`gamTrack()`, `gamCP()`); the slot scale couples those cells to moderator blocks, the absorber and
  control-channel cells by the faces the slots share (`gamChain()`, Wigner's rational law on the blocks
  and the absorber); the chain is solved directly and conserves to round-off (23/09/26). The shares are
  solved at design time on a 5 × 5 (void × rod coverage) grid, prompt and decay heat each, and
  interpolated per node. The water's share goes straight into the channel (`csDQ`), and so do the
  structures' and the absorber's; the control channels' share goes into their own water (`csCQ`); every
  drawn block has a temperature of its own and releases its share through a conductance off the drawing
  (`graphCellOf()`): the fuel columns to their channels, the control-channel columns (`csNTgC`) to their
  own cold water, and each to the other sideways across the gap between columns. A bored stack also
  conducts between neighbouring nodes, radially through block and column gap, axially through the blocks
  (`eCoreSpread()`). Rated power is the pin
  limit divided by the pin's share.
  `docs/fidelity.md` rows "heat deposited outside the fuel" and "the control absorber is drawn".
- **Energy is integrated.** `eAdvectStep()` carries m·h, boron and hydrogen conservatively on the donor
  edges and adds the flow work V·dp, so U = m·h − p·V is conserved; the tick's energy residual is
  `SC_ENRES`. The old step relaxed each node's enthalpy toward its inflow mean and dropped V·dp.
- **The turbine does the isentropic drop** of its inlet steam to the exhaust, times a stage efficiency of
  0.85 (`eTurbDh()`); shaft power `eMwE()`, generator `eMWe()` (× 0.985). The stated plant efficiency is
  no longer an input.
- **Room gas is a mixture:** a cell's pressure is n·R·T/V over the moles of air, steam and hydrogen
  (`eRoomMolFill()`), not every kilogram at air's gas constant.
- **Oxidation on a burst pin is two layers**, inner and outer face, each parabolic (`csNOxI`).
- **A spring valve lifts on its own inlet pressure**, after a vessel breach too (`eSpringStep()`).
- **A stage's water capacity rate is the secant of h(T)** over the tube (`eStageSecant()`).
- **The settle** (`engSettle()`) repeats the tube fit and the shell walk until they agree, seeds a dead
  leg with the water of the line it hangs off, puts a vacuum condenser at its own heat balance, and dials
  boron on the final field.
- The checks against physics are `node tests/physics/run.js`.

Point kinetics (6 groups, implicit Euler, 4 substeps/20 ms) over a **nodal core**: 14 rings × 10
levels, 1-group diffusion, its fundamental mode solved exactly by shifted inverse iteration (`fluxSolve()`). Kinetics owns total power; the field owns shape and weighted
feedback. **Fuel temp and void are the field** — the pin is a heat balance and void is quality off
the enthalpy carried up each channel. No lump, no rescale, no cap, no dry-cooling floor.

- **Xenon/iodine and samarium on a 100× clock** (`XE_CLOCK`) — the xenon peak arrives ~5.4 min after a scram, and samarium only grows after one. The central mechanic.
- **Per-channel flow**: a voiding channel loses the flow it needed to stop voiding (the Chernobyl loop). The strength of it is the **homogeneous two-phase friction multiplier**, `phi2 = 1 + x·(1/rvl − 1)`, and parallel channels at equal pressure drop carry `w ∝ 1/√phi2` — published, not a typed gain. It reads QUALITY (`voidQual()` runs `driftFlux` backwards, so no second array on `S`) and it reads `rvl`, so a depressurising core loses channel flow harder. The separated-flow form is what to reach for if this ever reads too weak; `P.dh` is already there for it.
- **The pin is a balance** (`eng/core.js`): power in, film out (`(mflux·chW)^0.8·(1−nV)`), the fuel stepped in enthalpy on the drawn fuel (`latFuelKg()`) and each fuel row's own h(T), UO2 on Fink, a metal on its phase law with its latent heats (`eFuelHA()`/`eFuelTA()`), and `pinUA` is the drawing's conduction, pellet + gap + clad + film per metre (`pinRes()`), at the rest flow's film (`eCorePinFit()`); τ is a result. A dry node has no film and climbs on its own heat capacity until it melts. `P.TfRef` is the flat mean pellet at rest off the same resistances (`pinDTf()`, `tfRefOf()`).
- **T-AVG IS THE AVERAGE.** The channel rise is computed FIRST and the channel centred on `s.Tavg`. Hang the inlet off last tick's rise and the flux-weighted mean coolant temperature moves with power at −45 pcm/K, prompt: measured, the plant diverged in a quarter of a second.
- **Void is quality; `s.vf` is a MEASUREMENT.** `driftFlux(x)` is a correlation between two real quantities. `sc = vLump/raw` is gone.
- **`XMIX` is cross-flow, and it is the one fit left in the core.** A core is not fourteen sealed pipes: an open lattice mixes hard between assemblies, so a hot channel's enthalpy rise is far flatter than its power (real PWR: Fq ≈ 2.5, enthalpy-rise factor ≈ 1.55). Without it the stock centre ring — flux 2.20 against a mean of 1 — boils at 83 % power and locks into its own runaway at rest. The PELLET is not mixed.
- **`s.coreDT` is MEASURED**, not lagged onto a fit. `CORE_DT_TAU` is gone; `coreDTMax()` stays, because past it the channel is boiling rather than getting hotter and buoyancy is the one thing that reads it. It starts at `coreDT0()·P.n0`, not 0.
- **DNBR IS W-3 — FOR A FLUID THAT DOES DNB.** Tong (1967), the published correlation, evaluated in the paper's own units and converted once at the door (`dnbW3()`, `step.js`). It wants real heat flux, real mass flux, quality, hydraulic diameter and pressure, and after the bundle got pins all five are measured — `P.aHeat`, `P.G0`, `s.xHot`, `P.dh`, `s.pCore`. The five invented exponents (`hotFlow^.6`, `(p/P0)^.3`, `(sc/20)^.4`) and the hand-picked 2.66 peaking factor are gone. **W3_LIM is the correlation's OWN domain**, not a guard invented here: past it the first bracket's exponential runs away and a core with MORE steam reads MORE margin. Since 24/09/26 (batch K) no law carries a constant: W-3 and the rest read raw, a boiling core reads CISE-4's critical power ratio (`E_DNB_CPR`), and `P.dnbr0` is commissioning's rest reading of each core's own law.
- **THERE IS MORE THAN ONE THERMAL LIMIT, AND `dnbLaw` IS WHICH ONE.** Sodium at 0.2 MPa sits 430 K below saturation and never departs from nucleate boiling; helium never boils at all. Clamping them to W-3's low pressure edge gave them water's shape in pressure and then stopped moving — plausible, and wrong. `dnbLaw` is one column on `COOLANT` and `dnbrOf()` is the one dispatch: **`w3`** for the three water rows, **`boil`** for sodium and salt (`(tsat(pCore) − Tin) / (s.coreDT·s.fq)` — how far the inlet sits below saturation over what the hot channel is actually doing to it), **`temp`** for helium (the same shape against `P.tdmg`, off `s.TfHot`, the peak node of the field the damage block already reads). No new typed constant in either. Each law's design limit is one table, `DNB_LIM` (batch K).
- **THE PIN DIAMETER IS A KNOB** (`D.rodD`, `?? rodDSuggest()` = 9.5 mm; `rodD()`, `lattice.js`). `latFuelFrac()`, `latRodFrac()`, the flow and heated areas, `D_h`, `P.cladKg`, `burstR()` and the pin's resistances and UO2 mass (`pinRes()`, `latFuelKg()`) all follow it. A thin pin buys surface — so a higher ceiling (see the rating below) — and stores less heat into a melt; it also carries more clad, which EATS: `ZR_ABS·modClad()` is clad volume over fuel volume at 1000 pcm, 291 pcm on the stock pin, and without it a thin pin was free. `latWarn()` HARD-refuses under 2 mm of gap on the 12.6 mm rod pitch.
- **THE PEAKING FACTOR IS THE RATING.** `D.power = latRating()`: the tighter of two ceilings on ONE pin, each over its own design margin — `qMelt = 4π·f.kint` (the fuel row's conductivity integral to its limit: UO2 6.3 kW/m to melt) over `MELT_M` = 21/13.6 (a centreline-melt limit of 21 kW/ft, CE System 80, over the Westinghouse 13.6 kW/ft design peak), and `qDnb = COOLANT[].qpp·π·rodD·fin` (the surface flux the coolant's law allows, MW/m²) over W-3's design limit 1.30 (`DNB_LIM.w3`, Tong 1967), on every core since `qpp` is a W-3-style figure — times every pin in the core, times the column, over `Fq`. So ZONE, POISON, REFLECTOR, the section and the pin all move the topbar number, and a flatter core makes more power out of the same fuel. **`PEAK_M` (1.283, solved to land the stock PWR on 1198 MWt) is gone, 23/09/26**; nothing is pinned to any MWt. The stock PWR binds on DNB at a rated peak of 12.60 kW/ft, against a Westinghouse 4-loop's 12.64–13.6 (NRC HRTD sec. 2.2). `COOLANT[].qpp` is still a bought column, not sourced. `COOLANT[].qpp` REPLACED the kW/L column (`dens` stays only as the fluid's own density stand-in for `rhoDesign()`), and power density is a READOUT. `P.dnbr0` is commissioning's rest reading of the core's own crisis law (batch K), and the bench row is BINDING LIMIT — which ceiling won and by how much the other is clear.
- **LEAKAGE IS MEASURED OFF THE SHAPE.** `coreLeak()` (`core2d.js`) sums the boundary current of the CONVERGED flux — `(1−ghost)·coupling·phi` on the rim, lid and floor, flux-and-volume weighted — on the same solve `coreFq()` makes, so rods, poison, `nPen` and the zone loading shape it first. On a uniform core it is M²B² exactly (the eigenvalue), and `flux.js` grades it against the analytic bare cylinder on the extrapolated dimensions to 0.4 %. **`LEAK_K` is gone (23/09/26):** it was 0.13 ≈ (7.6/21)², a calibration that cancelled the 21 cm migration length; with a real M (`latMig()`) the raw term is the leakage. The stock PWR leaks 2.8 % of its neutrons (2896 pcm), a large PWR under 3 % and a 160 MWt iPWR over 7 % (Krall et al., PNAS 2022).
- **THE BUNDLE HAS PINS.** A can off the core's `CLAD` row (Zircaloy 0.57 mm, Magnox 1.83 mm) on the core's own rod pitch (`rodPOf()`, 12.6 mm suggested), a fin area ratio on the film (`finOf()`), and `latFuelFrac()` (pellet), `latRodFrac()` (what displaces coolant), the flow and heated areas and `D_h` are all arithmetic off those and the drawn diameter (`latBundle()`, `lattice.js`). Opening the lattice now adds flow area without adding rod surface, which is the pitch dependence the typed `XSUB_AR` could not express — and both Saha-Zuber branches are measured off the drawn bundle, height and power density included.

- **A FLUID HAS ITS OWN SATURATION CURVE, AND ITS OWN LATENT HEAT.** `satN` was a typed `.10` on every row — one curve shape for water, sodium, salt and helium — and `XHFG` was water's 1500 kJ/kg dividing every family's quality term. Exothermic at `ZR_QOX`, entering the pellet balance as `W/(nodeW[k]·P.pinUA)` — the `nodeW` cancellation is exactly the `qhat*pw` currency and is verified by probe to machine precision. Gated on `COOLANT.oxid` (the three water rows), on `OX_T0 = 1073 K` and on node void: **exactly zero at every preset's rest point**, which it must be or `commission()`'s one real `step()` anchors `P.dnbrK` against a core that was quietly burning. `s.h2` is the inventory IN THE PRIMARY; what leaves the loop leaves with the steam, at the same openings, and burns in the room (see "Heat is a place too"). Receipts: the `docs/fidelity.md` rows "molten core material moves down", "the vessel's lower head under a melt", "corium outside the vessel".

The end state is the node minimum; the two are apart until that switch is made.

- **The core stays axisymmetric.** Throttling one loop lowers total flow; it cannot tilt the core. Per-loop asymmetry lives in the generators.

**Pressure is a place, not a number** (`pipenet.js`, `net.js`). Node = `partId+side`, edge = a
routed run. Every edge states a real flow COEFFICIENT `C = A/√K` (m², `pipeC()`) and every node a
real density off its own `(p, h)`, so one momentum relation `w = C·√(2·ρ_u·dp_eff)` returns
**kilograms per second** and enters the matrix as `g = w/dp`, relinearised about last tick's field.
Banded LDLᵀ on a reverse Cuthill-McKee order, one factorisation per solve. `netFixed()` names the
KNOWN nodes (containment, each shell's pool, a tank with no charge, an `inf` tank), each at its own
ABSOLUTE pressure; a charged tank, a hold tank's bubble and a steam space are STORES, not fixed. The static
head is an explicit per-edge term at the edge's own mean density (`staticH()`).

- **A BOILING PRIMARY HOLDS ITS OWN PRESSURE, AND THE DATUM IS A SATURATION CURVE.** `P.steam` (`P.sc0 <= 0`, measured at commissioning) is the one predicate: a loop whose hot leg is already at saturation is its own steam space and needs no pressurizer, so `s.P` follows the loop's temperature up and down its own curve (`satSlope(P.sat, s.P)·s.dTavg`, the exact derivative of `satT`/`satP`) instead of chasing a `Pdem` affine in `s.Tavg` through a 3.3 s lag. **Boiling pressurises now** — the one strong negative feedback a boiling core has, and without it Doppler was the only thing opposing a positive void coefficient. A RATE, not the absolute curve: RBMK programmes 550 K against `tsat0` 558, so the absolute form would re-anchor every plant off its design pressure, and every hole (blowdown, relief, break) goes on moving `s.P` untouched. A subcooled plant takes the old branch and is **bit-identical to 17 significant figures** — PWR, SFR, MSR and HTGR all verified.
- **THE CURRENCY IS KILOGRAMS.** `PIPE_K`, `INJ_K`, `BREAK_K`, `FEED_LEN`, `BUOY_LIN` and the `LOOP_TRANSIT` conversion are all gone, and with them the property this file was built around: the graph used to be homogeneous in `PIPE_K`, so a solved flow was a RATIO and kilograms arrived through one conversion off the PRIMARY loop's inventory — which is why a condensate line metered against the reactor's pipes read 799 kg/s of water for 664 kg/s of steam. `netKgs(q)` is now `|q|` and `invRate(q)` is `100·q/loopKg()` — and **`loopKg()` is MEASURED now, not fitted**: it is `P.invKg0`, this plant's own commissioned water summed off the settled field over the core piece's unbooked nodes (91 085 kg on the stock ship, where the old `LOOP_TRANSIT`/`coreDT0()` correlation said 87 094). The correlation survives only as the answer for a design with no commissioned network to ask, which is the bench. **`PIPE_K`-invariance is dead on purpose**; any sweep asserting it is asserting the old model.
- **`netKappa()` is checked against the tables.** `netKapF` reads `BETA_W`/`solidK` = 0.0025/1.4 = 1.79e-3 per MPa for pressurised water, the right order for 583 K; a steam space at 6.8 MPa reads 0.147 per MPa against water's 0.0018, which is why a shell's steam node dominates its own compliance.
- **`CASING_F` IS A CASING LOSS NOW (0.05), NOT A FIT.** The loop spends its head on real losses (M3, `docs/report-physics-validation.md` section 7): Haaland friction on the run's own Reynolds number at the donor's viscosity (`fricOf`, `COOLANT[].mu`/`muV`, `SAT_WATER.mu`), an elbow per direction change the router drew and a nozzle at each end (`runK0`, `K_BEND`/`K_ENTRY`/`K_EXIT`), and each machine's stated internal loss (`ROLE.internal[].K` on a generator's and an exchanger's tubes, `ROLE.core.kEnd` for the vessel's plena per leg). `CASING_F` is what a pump's own body costs at its rated duty - a twentieth of `PUMP_H0` - and moving it 0.25 to 0.05 moved the stock reference 3 956 to 4 020 kg/s.
- **Buoyancy is the static head, and there is no anomaly left in it.** `staticH()` is `ρ̄·g·Δz` at the edge's own two node densities, in ABSOLUTE pressure. `phiRef()`/`rhoDatum()`/`buoyH()`/`BUOY_LIN` are gone: they existed because the network was one liquid at one density, and a datum is not computable with a density per node. **What that gives up**: against the datum an isothermal loop's static heads summed to exactly 0.0 by construction, so a manufactured thermosiphon was impossible; now they sum to zero only as well as the density field does. Elevation still comes from the GRID (`p.y`, `MPC`), never `rowTop()`.
- **A break is a hole, not a blockage.** A severed run gains **two** break edges to a containment node at `P.Pcont`. A hole is `holeC(bore)` — a sharp-edged orifice, `Cd·A`, no length term — and the choke is in the one relation, so no constant is fitted for it. **The choke is not a vapour special case**: `dp_eff` is capped at `(1-RCRIT)` of the HIGHER of the two pressures (never the donor's — a pump takes suction at a tenth of a bar and read off the donor the cap choked the stock feed pump to 176 kg/s of a 636 kg/s duty).
- **GRAVITY IS NOT A PUMP, AND THE CHOKE GATE ASKS ONLY ABOUT PUMPS (07/09/26).** `flowG()`'s gate is `!hSrc && F.x[up] > 0`, and `ed.hSrc` is the head SOURCE alone — written beside `ed.h` in `netFinish()`, off the same closure, `HEAD_K` and all. `ed.h` stays the TOTAL and is what drives the flow; only the gate reads the source. It was `!h`, and `h` carries `staticH()`, so an edge could choke only where its two ends sat at exactly the same height: on the stock plant that held the cap off the turbine's own swallow (`vap:turb`, dp 6.22 MPa against a 3.05 cap, held off by 0.65 kPa of column) and off its exhaust (`exh:condt-turbb`, 0.551 against 0.247, held off by 13 kPa). A real turbine IS choked at its first stage. The gate reads the source's **value this tick**, not whether the edge could have one, so a stopped pump is a length of pipe and free to choke — measured, the standby feed pump's edge reads `hSrc` 0.000000 stopped against 1.46 running. **What it cost:** the commissioning governor refit absorbs it (`P.turbC` +39 to +49 % on every preset, stock 0.028912 → 0.041177) and every plant rests where it did — stock 60 s MWe 373.23 → 372.11, shell 6.9051 → 6.9071 MPa, `T-avg` 583.171 → 583.177 K, primary 15.5116 → 15.5120 MPa.
- **THE CIRCULATION REFERENCE IS THE NOMINAL PLANT, NOT THE DRAWN ONE.** `P.netRef` is solved on `P.netNom` — same arrangement, same gates, same lengths, every run and every fitting at the bore it SHIPS at (`withNomBore()`) — and on its OWN converged pump duty (`P.pumpQNom`). It is **kilograms per second** now, not an abstract current. **Re-measured after the momentum law**, stock one-loop ship: `P.netRef` 6719.2 kg/s (was 4.9342 abstract) and unmoved by the nodal work since, `netFlowK` 1.1229 (1.1543 until the secondary came alive in the reference solve at all - see the ordering rule in `.claude/CLAUDE.md`). Cold-leg bore sweep: 1200 mm 1.436, 750 mm 1.154, 600 mm 0.953, 300 mm 0.308, 150 mm 0.061 (was 1.3426 / 1.0310 / 0.8507 / 0.3514 / 0.1048) — the same shape, steeper, because the answer is now a root in the differential. Per-RUN references (`P.netRefByRun`, the meter scales) stay on the drawn plant — a run is metered against what it actually carries.
- **A RUN BURSTS AT ITS OWN WALL.** `runBurstP() = runRating() × 1.5`, the same margin the shell carries (`SG_BURST_K`), because an allowable stress already has its safety factor in it. `step()` asks every run every tick and cuts ONE cell into `s.dmgParts` — the hole a hit makes, so nothing about the plume, the inventory, the repair or the ledger is new. WHERE is `DICE.burstCell`, rolled at the burst, uniform over that run's cells (seeds 1–4 on a 4 mm cold leg: `22,26` / `24,26` / `24,26` / `27,25`); `s.diceOff` takes the pushing end. Stock plant, 4 mm on the cold leg: rating 0.37 MPa, bursts at 0.55, splits on tick 1, 86.7 % inventory at 5 s, LOW FLOW trip. **It exposed two things that had been wrong while a wall was only a mass**: `pAt()` answers `s.P` for anything the liquid solve does not carry, so every steam line was judged against the reactor's 15.5 MPa (it reads `s.vapP` for those now, and a run with no pressure at either end is not judged); and a feed line was walled for the shell it feeds (6.9 MPa) while its own pump discharges into that shell at 9.9, so the stock condensate line cut itself in 4 s — `runDesignP()` takes the GREATER of `sgDesignP()` and `feedHeadMax()`, never the sum. Feed/condensate wall 31 → 43 mm, plant mass 1724.8 → 1722.7 t.
- **A WRECKED MACHINE IS AN OPEN CIRCUIT, NOT A LENGTH OF PIPE.** Every component's internal edge is gated on its own damage id, and `g<=0` is an absent edge, so an undamaged plant assembles the identical matrix. Before this only a pump noticed being destroyed (it lost its head, kept its casing), so water circulated between two wrecks. A hit RCP takes the stock loop from 7572 kg/s to 0 and trips on LOW FLOW — **on a one-loop ship that kills natural circulation too, which is the cost of sealing the wreck rather than opening it**; the alternative (a break edge to containment per wrecked node) was considered and not taken. A tube-ruptured generator is unaffected in the way that matters: the SGTR edge is built elsewhere and still reaches the primary face, so the leak runs with nothing flowing through the machine. `netFactored()`'s key carries the whole `s.dmgParts` list now, which subsumes the severed-cell and ruptured-generator fields it used to carry apart.
- **PIPEWORK AND PORTS ARE DAMAGE TARGETS.** Neither is in `LAY.parts`, which is exactly what they had been falling through: the heat-damage integral walked that list, so a run threading a compartment hot enough to wreck every machine in it never failed (`PIPE_TSURV` 900 K against the AIR at its own cell — what a run carries is its own fluid and says nothing about the fire around it; injected at 1100 K it cuts in 25 s = `ROOM_DMG_TAU`), and a PORT could not be hit at all. Both go through the pseudo-part `dmgPart()` already returned for a pipe cell. A wrecked port jams where it stood: `ACT.portShut` refuses through the row's `part`, so the position is simply never changed and there is no new flag on `S`.
- **Cavitation is a head loss at each pump's own suction** (`s.cavP`, keyed by PUMP), not a scalar on total flow. Begins when subcooling reaches zero on that node's OWN circuit curve; `scAt(node)` says where, and it fills and clears over `CAV_TAU`.
- **A tube rupture is a differential leak** — an edge to a node fixed at that generator's own secondary pressure, so depressurising the primary slows it and stops it at equalisation.

**Pressurizer level is the vessel node's own void fraction** (`holdLvlOf()`), and its pressure is the
saturation pressure of what is in it: the vessel is a two-phase node of the field. `expSrc`/`netExpSurge`
and `LVL_K` are gone. The real check is memory: two levels a tick apart must stay apart.

**THE SHELL IS ONE STATE, AND IT IS A PRESSURE** (`s.sgPBy`, A6). It used to be a saturated POT
carrying a water mass, a steam mass, a steam volume AND a temperature — so a pressure by the
equation of state and a second one off the saturation line, reconciled by a fitted four-second
`SG_FLASH_TAU`. In a saturated vessel those cannot disagree, so the state is collapsed.
**GONE**: `SG_FLASH_TAU`, `sgSteamEq()`, `sgSteamKg()`, `secP()`'s two-source reconciliation and
the `potStep()` on `s.sgTBy`. `secPTarget()` survives as **the fallback only**, for a caller with no
live shell — the bench, `layoutMetrics()`, the tick-zero seed.

**AND SINCE B1 IT IS A ROW OF THE MATRIX.** A6 walked that state as a first-order lag in `step()`:

```
dp/dt = (Q_tubes − fed·cp·(Tsat − T_feed) − skin − hfg·(steam that left))
        / (sgHeatCap·dTsat/dp + hfg·sgCapAt)
```

Divided through by `hfg` that is exactly one node's KCL row with a storage term, and that is where
it lives now. `shellStoreC()` is the diagonal — `sgCapAt()` plus `sgHeatCap·dTsat/dp / hfg`, the
whole of what resists the pressure moving, in kg/MPa — and `shellStoreW()` is the injected current,
the tube heat less the feedwater's own way up to saturation, the skin and the safety valves, over
`hfg`. `netStore()` (`pipenet.js`) hands both to `netAssemble()`'s `cap` argument and to the
existing `src` array. The pressure is READ off the field afterwards; `s.sgTBy` is `tsatSec(p)` and
`s.sgSteamBy` is what the space holds at it, both DERIVED and both still written for their readers
and for the ledger. **What BOILED is taken off the derived charge**, `(ms_new − ms)/dt` plus what
left, never off `sgCapAt·dp` — the space also moves with the water level under it, and pricing the
boil off the compliance alone left 0.44 kg a tick unattributed.

**THE FEED TRAIN LANDS ON A POOL, AND THAT IS WHAT MADE IT POSSIBLE.** A6 tried the compliance
directly and could not have it: what landed on the steam node was the FEED, whose water goes into
the pool at the bottom of the shell, so the storage term had to subtract a lagged flow — and a
lagged flow makes the solve force that edge to carry what it carried last tick. Measured then: the
feed doubled every commissioning pass and the node ran to −10 988 MPa. `ROLE.sg`'s secondary
internal path now ends at `sec:<id>`, the same node the tube rupture already leaks into, because it
is the same water. That node is FIXED at the shell's own pressure, one tick old, exactly as
`tankP()` and `condP()` are — a pool of water has no compliance worth a matrix row, and it has to
stay a boundary anyway, because `holdLive()` and `circSolid()` read the fixed map to mean
"somewhere the water can go". Nothing in the storage row is then a flow this solve also decides.

**A GENERATOR'S STEAM NODE IS REGISTERED WHETHER OR NOT A STEAM RUN ROUTED.** With no edges at all
its row is `p = p_prev + w·dt/C`, which is the integral `step()` used to run — a shell with nowhere
to send its steam pressurises onto its own safeties, and that has to keep working. `netReadP()`
therefore refuses to FLOAT a piece containing a storing node: a store carries `C/dt·p_prev` and has
an absolute pressure of its own.

- **The secondary is WATER whatever the primary is.** `tsatSec()`/`psatSec()` (`pipenet.js`) is its own curve, two real steam-table points and an exact inverse. `satT()`/`satP()` is the shared curve either side calls; `tsat()` (`step.js`) is the PRIMARY coolant's and using it here put an SFR's secondary saturation 480 K ABOVE its primary. There is no `psat()` — the primary never needs the inverse.
- **Heat crosses on `Tavg − sgTemp`**, not on demand: `P.sgUA · flow^0.8 · sgFill · the tube stream's own two-phase film (`eFilmA()`, fidelity row two-phase film on an exchanger)`. Every old multiplier is a property of the CONDUCTANCE. `sgSteams()` and `condFrac()` have LEFT the heat path — they back the steam up instead of stopping the boiling. `s.load` is what the turbine swallows, nothing more.
- **`P.sgUA` and `P.swallow` are fitted at ONE anchor** (`commission()`): rated flow, design shell pressure, today's removal. The turbine is sized for the plant it was bought for, or the governor demands rated steam from a loop that carries 82 % of it.
- **A lid is a FITTING, and a shell without one BURSTS.** `secVent[id]` is the valves actually placed on that shell, each passing its share of `cap`, booked per valve as `s.reliefSteam[v.fid]`; `s.sgBurst[id]` is a shell that had none, and a burst is an opening at `SG_RELIEF_CAP` that does not reseat. The old plant-wide `SG_SAFE_K` is deleted — it was an invisible, unbreakable, un-bypassable machine with no box, no bore, no panel and no `AUTOSYS` row. Vented mass and its latent heat both leave, so a generator held on its valves uncovers its own tubes.
- **A full shell takes no more feedwater.** The level clamp used to swallow it, and the sensible-heat term then cooled a generator with water that was not there.
- **THE BYPASS IS A LOAD-FOLLOWING MACHINE ON EVERY PLANT.** `tProg()` is FLAT under `P.steam` (`P.Tref` at every load; the scram/runback branch is unchanged, and at full load the two expressions give the same number), and `dump` is live at part load rather than gated on `s.scrammed`. **`dumpLive` is DELETED**: it was `s.scrammed || P.steam`, so on a subcooled plant `dumpT` was computed and thrown away — measured sitting at 0.22 unread while the shell safeties lifted. The rest-point error `s.Tavg − tProg(s)` is exactly 0 at commissioning and negative at 120 s on all six presets, so the clamp gives a literal 0 and rest is bit-identical. **It is not enough on its own**: `DUMP_K = 0.02` per kelvin caps a stock PWR's dump near 0.24 against a 0.70 load step, so the shell still reaches its set point and the safeties still lift briefly (stock PWR to `loadDem` 0.30, dice off: first lift at 4 s, peak 718 kg/s, reseated and level recovered by 150 s; `loadDem` 0.85 never lifts). What stopped the compartment cooking is the vent, not the bypass. A `dumpP` pressure mode for subcooled plants is the machine that would hold the shell, and is a plan of its own. The reserve's flat `0.08` stays scram-gated — it is proportional to nothing. **And the signal is SHELL PRESSURE, not the programme error**: measured, a flat-programme BWR stepped to 70 % lifts its shell safeties in 12 s while `Tavg` has moved 0.03 K, and gains of 0.02, 0.1, 0.5, 2 and 10 per kelvin ALL lift them, because the fault is on the shell and the primary is the one thing still fine. `sgOverFrac()/sgBypBand()` is the pressure regulator a real BWR has; the band is DERIVED from the lowest secondary safety valve actually drawn (`PORV_LIFT0` if none), so regulator-first-safeties-last falls out of where the valves were set. At rest every shell sits below its design pressure, so the term clamps to exactly 0 on all six presets. `DUMP_K = 0.02` per kelvin keeps the scram path on its old signal.
- Steam raised is `sgQ/H_FG`; feedwater is what the condensate system puts back. Below `SG_DRY` the
  tubes uncover and that generator stops cooling the core.

- **Feedwater is SOLVED**, arriving through the generator's own shell (`outs.sgFeedBy[id]`, off the shell edge, never a run key). A feed pump develops the same head every pump develops — its own stated MPa, at its own speed, less its own cavitation — so it has to BEAT the shell rather than cancel it, and `pumpHeadSuggest()` sizes it at `PUMP_MARGIN` over the standing difference its own circuit carries.
- **One pump and one header cannot hold two generators at level; the regulating valve can.** `s.fregBy[id]` is a **HEAD, not a conductance** — so a moving valve cannot bust `netFactored()`'s signature, and cannot make the Laplacian indefinite. Wide open is exactly 0. Its error is **relative** to what that machine is boiling off, so one gain serves every generator type.
- **`FEED_LEN` is fitted once** and is the price of a regulating train. Static, so it never enters the cache signature.
- Heat crosses per loop (`sgShare()`, off `netFlowK`'s `outs.byLoop`), so a starved loop boils down slower. `sglMin()` is the one shared number. `secP(s, id)` is each generator's own secondary pressure; `sgInertiaK()` is the one inertia helper.
- **`SGT[].water` is read off real machines** — 55 t U-tube (Westinghouse Model F class) vs 7 t once-through (B&W OTSG class). Measure the drain ratio over **3 s, not 20**.
- **The hotwell is a tank with no `cell`**, so no node and no edge — it is condensate inside the condenser, drawn by it (`hostedTankIds()`). Capacity is 1.5× the generators' charge and it overflows ~45 s into a lost-feedwater event. Losing suction is CAVITATION at the pump (`s.cavP`), not a taper written about a tank — a ceiling anywhere would discard mass the solve already moved.
- **The circuit and the reserve are told apart by a RULE, not a name**: a secondary tank standing open all the time IS the circuit. **EFW starts on low level** (`AUTORULE.sglow`), not on being armed — measure it at **15 s, not 120**.

**ONE TANK. No kinds, no presets.** A tank is an elevation, a level, a pressure, one edge, and
contents with an activity. Source or sink is which way the solved differential points. A passive
tank works in a blackout and a pumped one does not.

- Config is `D.tanks[id]`, so it rides `designSig()`, the recording head and the save file free. `mass` follows from `vol` (`tankMass()`); `netBuild()` writes `net.tankNid`. A `cell` is what gives a tank a node; `side` only picks the currency of `tankKg()`.
- A gas-charged tank on the core's circuit keeps one state, its node's mass: the level is `100·m/tankKg` and the pressure is the polytropic law off that level, and both seeds (`eSeedKgA()`) put `level0·tankKg` in it.
- `FLUID` is substances, not components (`act`, `boron`, `temp`) — deliberately **no density row**.
- `AUTORULE` is when a tank opens itself: `manual`, `always`, `sglow`, `plow`.
- `tankLive()` is the one predicate the edge conductance, the cache key and the view declutter read.
- `layFit` covers the tank AND fitting sets (`laySrcSig() = checkSig() + tankSig() + fittingSig()`). `fittingSig()` carries the MODE as well as the cell, because a mode decides both the fold and whether the internal path is a gate.
- **Boron is a fluid, not a button.** `FLUID.borated.boron` is pcm per 1 % of inventory delivered; N boron tanks are additive; a tank charged below loop pressure cannot inject at all.
- Every tank's rupture disc is `D.tanks[id].burst`; what it dumps costs release in proportion to `FLUID.act`.
- **A relief fitting fills nothing directly** — the tank's own node carries the current. `net.fitTarget` answers only "is there a tank to catch this at all".

**THE TURBINE AND THE CONDENSER ARE MACHINES.** `mwE` is steam times an enthalpy drop
(`turbDh(secP, condP)`, `P.hTurb` fitted so the drop at the design point is exactly `H_FG`), and
`P.eff` still prices the machine you bought. `condPen()` is **deleted**, hand-picked 0.6 floor and
all.

- **A CAPACITY SLIDER'S FLOOR IS A MACHINE THAT FAILS, AND ITS MASS IS PRICED OFF THE CAPACITY.** Every span used to be affine about "adequate plus a bit" — `turbSwallow` 105–145 %, `condDuty` 85–120 %, `pumpCap`/`ihxCap` 70–130 % — so the smallest turbine still swallowed more than rated, the smallest condenser kept up with a plant resting at 85.7 %, and max was free. They are centred on their old midpoints and opened out: swallow **45–205 %**, duty **30–175 %**, dump **0–100 %**, pump and exchanger **25–175 %**, `turbEff` **×0.70–1.30**. A default plant is bit-identical by construction. Mass follows the capacity (`turbSwallow(v)*TURB_MASS`, `condDuty(v)*COND_MASS`, as the pump always did), never the slider position, or widening a span hands out the extra machine for nothing. **`turbEff()` and `condDump()` exist because one slider drives TWO quantities each** and both had a copy of the literal.
- **The condenser is a pot too** (`s.condT`): steam arriving in, and out through a CIRCULATING WATER MACHINE. **`COND_DT0 = 13 K` is the design terminal difference and `P.condUA` is fitted on it, so DUTY DIVIDES the terminal difference** — that is the whole range: 30 % duty sits at 331 K / 0.0155 MPa and 175 % at 299 K / 0.0047 MPa, worth 315 against 344 MWe on the stock plant. `COND_P0 = 0.004 MPa` is the FLOOR and it is the AIR IN-LEAKAGE limit alone, so it sits BELOW the design point (`condPDes()`, `RAD_TDES + COND_DT0`) rather than on it; `P.hTurb` anchors on the design point too. Fitted onto the floor instead, every healthy condenser pinned to it and the entire slider was worth 1 % of output. `RAD_TDES = 307 K` is the CANONICAL REFERENCE SINK, not a reservoir temperature — see the radiator row. `condK` is bought capacity minus what is broken, minus drowned tubes, and it may be exactly 0: nothing divides by it, and a wrecked condenser rejecting nothing is the answer. **THE POOL'S TEMPERATURE IS ITS OWN ENTHALPY** — `condPoolT()` bisects the saturation curve for the T whose liquid-plus-forced-vapour enthalpy is the node's `h`, and `condSatP` is `psat` of that. `tOfH()` at a pinned pressure is NOT a pool reading: on the shelf it returns Tsat(p) and never looks at `h`, and `p` was set off last tick's T, so that pair carries no energy at all and ratchets down onto the `COND_P0` floor. `condTRead()` is the one door and it falls back to `netTempAt()` where the volume can hold no liquid.
- **THE CIRCULATING WATER IS A MACHINE, NOT A RESERVOIR.** `condRej` is the effectiveness form — `Q = cwC·(1−e^(−UA·condK/cwC))·(condT−s.cwInT)`, with `cwC` the water's heat capacity rate and `s.cwInT` the temperature the water actually ARRIVES at, off the field the panels have just chilled — wired to a panel's own temperature by role name, this machine rejected into whatever panels the plant owned whether or not one drop of water ran between them. The old `UA·(condT−T_CW)` (and `T_CW` is gone) was the ṁ→∞ limit of exactly this, so nothing was wrong; what it had no room for was an outlet temperature, pumps, or a way to fail. **`CW_RISE = 10 K` is the one new constant** — a real surface-condenser circulating-water rise — and `P.cwC` is derived from it at commissioning off the design point, riding the condenser slider with the duty and the dump (the SUM, as `P.condUA` does, not `condSizeMean()`'s mean). `P.condUA` re-anchors as `P.cwC·ln(COND_DT0/(COND_DT0−CW_RISE))`, which makes the effectiveness exactly `CW_RISE/COND_DT0` at full flow whatever was bought — so **every preset reads its design terminal difference and its old rest point bit-for-bit**, and only the shape away from full flow moved. `cwOut()` is the readout that says the sink is finite. `condK`'s old `blackout ? 0.25` is DELETED: the pumps sit on the main board, so `cwK` is 0 in a blackout and there is then no sink at all.
- **A TURBINE TRIPS ON BACKPRESSURE AND A CONDENSER IS NOT A PRESSURE VESSEL.** `TURB_TRIP_P = 0.02 MPa` is the real figure and about four times rated rejection, so no healthy plant is near it — **and it is now the ceiling the WHOLE radiator sizing is derived backwards from** (`tsatSec(0.02) = 338.6 K`, less an 18.6 K working margin, less `COND_DT0`, gives `RAD_TDES = 307`). Do not "fix" a tripping plant by moving it: that is reaching for the old numbers, and it would re-price every panel on the ship. `s.turbTrip` shuts the stop valve (`passK`). Past `COND_ATM = 0.101 MPa` the condenser has relieved: `s.condLost` is latched, the steam gate goes to 0, the sink's own `break:<id>` edge opens at `condVentBore()` (the ship's bypass steam share, choked at `COND_ATM`, the way `fitBoreSuggest()` sizes a spring), `s.condVent` is what the transport landed on that edge, `condP` sits at atmospheric and `s.condT` is capped at that saturation temperature. Neither latch resets — there is no pump on this plant that puts the air back. `s.condT`'s silent 900 K clamp is gone with them. **Seen to fail:** BWR blackout at full power — `condT` rises 1.35 K/s, turbine trips at 27.9 s, the bypass keeps feeding the condenser, vacuum is lost at 86.0 s and the shells then go to their safeties at 454 kg/s. On a PWR the same blackout trips the turbine at 16.3 s and the chain STOPS there, because a shut stop valve and no dump means no steam reaches the condenser at all.
- **THE SINK IS A RADIATOR, AND IT IS THE ONLY WAY HEAT LEAVES THIS SHIP.** This is a space game; there is nothing to reject into. The last leg is Stefan-Boltzmann exact (`SIGMA`, `T_SPACE = 3 K`), because the T⁴ IS the gameplay: a panel sits at `Q^(1/4)`, so the overload a plant can take is `(ceiling/RAD_TDES)^4` and **nobody types it — panel area buys it**. `radRejOf(s,id)` is the one per-instance expression the tick, the rail and the bench all read.
  - **AND THE CHAIN IS PLUMBED, END TO END.** A panel is a heat exchanger with space on one side, so it cools THE WATER IN IT and nothing else: `s.radQBy[id] = radUAOf(id)·(flow/ref)^0.8·(T_in − T_panel)`, off the solved inlet node, and `s.radTBy[id]` is that panel's own pot. On the stock ship that reads `s.sgTBy` → `s.condT` → circulating water → `s.radTBy` → space; splice a panel into a cold leg instead and the first two hops are simply not there. **Where the heat is DEBITED is the circuit** — `inCore()` and nothing else: on the core's it is `removal`, exactly as a generator's crossing is; on any other the pot that warmed that water has already been charged, and charging it twice is what a second book always does. The double entry into the enthalpy field is `advectSrc()`, the same shape `ROLE.sg` makes. **`RAD_DT0 = 8 K`** is the approach the tubes are bought at and `radUASuggest()` is that panel's own design rejection over it.
  - **A PANEL SEEDS ITS CIRCUIT, IT DOES NOT ANCHOR IT.** `advectAnchors()` returns `{hold, seed}` and the difference is load-bearing: a pot with a real heat term needs the seed and must not have the hold, or it reads its own answer back out of the field. The panel used to be both at once, which is how it came to show cold water leaving it while removing nothing from anything, and how a heat sink spliced anywhere but the condenser's circulating water was inert. `IN.anch` (`ROLE`) is the one declaration — a condenser's STEAM side holds, its circulating water does not. **No commissioned flow through a panel, no duty** (`P.netRefByRun`, `cwK`'s own guard): a pendant panel solves at 1e-14 of reference, and letting it take the 0.02 stagnant floor read 176 MW of sink off a dead leg.
  - **ANCHOR AT A CANONICAL REFERENCE SINK, OR THE RADIATOR IS FREE.** `P.hTurb`, `P.cwC`, `P.condUA` and `condPDes()` anchor at **`RAD_TDES = 307 K`**, the sink the machinery was DESIGNED for — a stated design point, the way a turbine is built for a stated exhaust pressure. Point them at `s.radTBy` instead and buying a terrible radiator moves the design point with it, the plant reads the same output, and the whole feature buys nothing. `s.radTBy` is what the plant HAS; `RAD_TDES` is what it was sold.
  - **IT MUST SEE SPACE.** `radLive(id)` = one perimeter cell with an outward neighbour on the skin, `hullCell()`, the same predicate `fitVentOut` asks. A blind panel has **`radArea` exactly 0** and is a hot surface in the room instead (`partTemp()`). Measured: the stock pair inboard puts the panels at 335.8 K against 297.0 K on the hull.
  - **`RAD_AREA_CELL = 62468 m²` IS THE ONE FUDGE, and with `graceK` deleted it is very nearly the only one left.** A cell is `MPC² = 0.218 m²`, so this lies by ~10⁵ — the same lie the ship's scale already tells (a 60-cell hull is 28 m and holds a 1 GW reactor), not a new one. Set ONCE off the stock plant's rated rejection (`P.rated*1000*(1−P.eff)`, the expression `P.cwC` already uses) at `RAD_TDES` on 30 cells of panel. **Do not tune it afterwards to recover output.** `RAD_MASS_M2` is priced against it for the same reason.
  - **`partTsurv(p)` is the ONE door to where a machine gives up**, because a coating scales it per instance (`RADCOAT`: bare metal 0.30/×1.15, white paint 0.85/×1.00, ceramic 0.94/×0.80). Two readers asking `ROLE.tsurv` directly would disagree with the panel that sold the coating.
- **The stop valve is a Stodola line**: `steamTo ∝ (Ps/Pdes)·√(1−(Pc/Ps)²)`. An isolated condenser, a flooded one and an overloaded one are then ONE behaviour — rejection down, backpressure up, enthalpy drop down, steam backs up, the shell pressurises — with no factor naming any of the three.
- **`net.condNode`'s fixed VALUE is live.** Only conductances enter `netFactored()`'s signature, so a moving fixed value cannot bust the cache.

**Radiation is a place, not a number** (`src/data/rad.js`). Each ray is `1/r²`, attenuated over the
exact chord through every cell. **There is no line-of-sight test — attenuation *is* the test.**
`radMu()` keys on what a component *is*, not `p.grp`.

- **Airborne release is a floor on every cell and is not shielded** — that is the containment argument made picture-shaped.
- `radSrc(null)` returns `core:1` **by fiat**, which is what makes `P.dose` purely geometric. **Do not scale it by `P.rated` or `P.n0`.** `RAD_K` is fitted once.
- `radAt()` is the worst seat in a room; `radParty()` the coldest free cell beside a job.
- `radWorkK(r)` slows a party in a hot field (never to zero); at 100 % dose the party is withdrawn. One helper, because the repair block does it and the panel promises it.

**Heat is a place too, and the ONE difference from radiation is MEMORY** (`src/data/room.js`).
`s.roomT` is a `Float64Array(GW*GH)` of kelvin **on `S`** — the `s.nDmg`/`s.nOx`/`s.nMelt` shape —
because a falloff kernel would make the room hot only while a machine is hot and would leave
nothing for a cooling machine to remove. `radSolve()` is resolved fresh; this is integrated. **The
"unless it is heavy to compute" clause is invoked out loud in the file**: real compartment transport
is plume-dominated and what is written is explicit diffusion with an upward bias, the `XMIX`
admission one level up.

- **`ROLE.thermal` is READ now** — `partTemp()` is the ONE door to what a machine's surface is at (`sgHot()` idiom), and a role that moves no heat has no surface. `ROOM_RUN_T` is the same answer per RUN KIND, because a pipe is not a part. A kind or role with no row invents no temperature.

What falls out and is typed nowhere: the plume delivers gas at `h/(ROOM_ENTRAIN·c_p)` ≈ 100 K above ambient for a 1 kg/s weep and a 600 kg/s rupture alike. Only the VOLUME differs. **AND IT IS NOT WELL MIXED**: the deposit was FLAT over every cell the flood reached, so a release big enough to size its plume at the whole compartment warmed all 2040 cells by the same amount in ONE tick and the break had no place at all (measured, severed hot leg: break cell +126.83 K, far corner +126.82 K). `roomSpread()` weights by `1/(1+ring)` off the breadth-first ring the cell was reached on — same break, same tick, **+2206.6 K at the opening, +1222.6 at its neighbours, +39.4 at the far corner**, and the front then walks outward one ring a tick. Both fields keep a discharge out of the room; **only the tank keeps it out of `s.release`** — a stack release is still loose in the environment, so `step()`'s release accounting is deliberately untouched and gates on `fitTarget` alone. The stock `sv0..sv3` sit at `y=1` and already faced the skin, so no plumbing moved and `P.netRef`, the node set and the 14 traced connections are unchanged. The old behaviour survives in the HOLE path and only there: `s.sgVentBy`'s residual — a burst shell or a severed steam line — is NOT gated on either field, because a hole has no set point, no bore and no stack.
- **THE SKIN RADIATES; IT IS NOT A CLAMP.** `T_CW` was ONE symbol doing THREE unrelated jobs (condenser sink, hull boundary, room floor) and is DELETED, not aliased — an alias is how the three got welded together. `RAD_TDES` (`step.js`) is the sink the machinery was designed for; `T_HULL = 293` (`room.js`) is now only what the ship was BUILT at and what the compartment starts from. A machine is a wall (`ROOM_BLOCK`), so a compact plant still runs hotter.

- **`ROOM_MIX = 0.35 m²/s` is the one fit, and since 20/09/26 it is a MASS exchange rate.** A face passes `ROOM_MIX·min(m_i,m_j)/MPC²` kg/s each way and what that carries is the two cells' own ENTHALPIES, which is the same form `eDiffuse()` already used for the species — one law, two callers.
